# claude-fable-5-2

Read this first: **this is not a blind run.** I designed the benchmark and wrote `BRIEF.md` in the same session, then built this game against it. I knew the rubric because I had just written it. Treat this entry as the worked example, and weigh it accordingly against runs that only ever saw the brief.

## What I built

**Gifts for the Tall One.** You are a cat on a small island. A human lives in the cottage and, as far as you can tell, cannot hunt. So you catch things and leave them on its doormat, and it pays you in treats while saying things like "That is GERALD. Put Gerald BACK."

- **The loop:** stalk, pounce, carry home, get paid, spend. Fifteen kinds of gift (ten animals with different senses and escape plans, four objects, and one red laser dot). The first of each kind pays triple, which pushes you around the whole island. Treats buy gear from the Cat-alogue on the porch (a backpack, quieter paws, longer pounces, night vision, water wings, a sprint), five coats, three hats, and a bell collar that the sparrows pay *you* to wear. The goal is a 150-treat tin of CATSLOP. Buying it plays the ending; you can carry on afterwards.
- **The cat** has no baked animation. Body, legs and tail are tubes rebuilt every frame from a small skeleton: hips, chest, head, four two-bone IK legs with paws planted on the terrain, and a verlet tail. Gait blends from walk through trot to gallop by speed; the spine bends into turns and flexes at a gallop; it crouches to stalk, wiggles before a pounce, stretches in the air, squashes on landing, paddles when swimming, shakes itself dry, and sits down with its tail wrapped round if you leave it alone. Pupils are slits by day and round at night or when hunting. Eyes glow in the dark.
- **The island** is one analytic height function sampled by everything (mesh, physics, water depth, grass, spawning): a cottage with a porch and garden, a clothesline, a pond with reeds and lily pads, a meadow with patches of tall grass that hide a sneaking cat, a wooded hill, beaches, a dock with a rowing boat, and a lighthouse. Wind-blown instanced grass bends away from the cat. There is a day and night cycle of nine minutes with different animals at different hours.
- **Sound** is all synthesised with Web Audio: meows, purring, footsteps that change on wood, sand and water, splashes, crickets, birdsong, frogs, the sea near the coast, and a small generative tune that shifts key at night.
- **No assets.** Every model, texture, icon and sound is generated by the code in `src/`. The journal's pictures are rendered from the game's own 3D models at start-up. The only dependency is the vendored three.js.

## How to play

`npm run serve`, open `/runs/claude-fable-5-2/`, press Enter.

| | |
|---|---|
| W A S D | walk (relative to the camera) |
| Mouse, or arrow keys left/right | look; wheel zooms |
| Space, tapped | hop (enough to catch a butterfly overhead) |
| Space, held | crouch and aim; a ring shows where you will land and turns gold when it locks onto something; release to pounce |
| C | sneak on/off. In tall golden grass a sneaking cat is all but silent |
| Shift | run |
| E | pick up objects, read the Cat-alogue on the porch bench |
| J | gift journal, which hints at where and when to find what you have not caught |
| M | meow |
| Esc or P | menu: volume, music, mouse speed, invert, graphics quality, erase progress |

Things can only be caught mid-pounce. Walk onto the doormat to deliver. Progress saves to `localStorage`. A gamepad and touch controls exist but see below.

## What I'm proudest of

1. The cat. A procedural rig was a risk compared with rigid boxes on a walk cycle, and it is what makes the game read as a cat rather than a mascot.
2. The pounce: hold to aim, a predicted arc and landing ring, gentle homing, a few frames of hit-stop on a catch. It is the one verb and it carries the game.
3. That the pieces answer each other: prey hearing depends on your speed, sneaking, the grass and your gear; the journal hints depend on the time of day; the Tall One's replies depend on what you brought and how many times.
4. Catching a mackerel in mid-air off the end of the dock and landing in the sea.

## What's missing or broken

Things I know:

- **Nobody has played it.** Every check was automated, on software rendering at a few frames per second. Control feel, camera feel, difficulty, pacing and the economy are designed and simulated, never felt. This is the largest unknown in the run.
- **I have never heard it.** The audio code runs without errors, but the test browser is muted. The meow in particular is a guess built from formant filters. Levels may be off.
- **Performance on a real GPU is unmeasured.** There is an automatic quality ladder (resolution, shadows, bloom, grass density) that steps down when the frame rate drops, and it is known to work on software GL, but I do not know where a given laptop lands.
- The cat cannot climb trees or get onto the cottage roof. It can get onto the porch, crates, the barrel, the fence, big rocks and the dock.
- Reloading the page returns you to the porch with an empty mouth; treats, purchases and the journal are kept.
- Prey that is out of season stays put if you are standing next to it, so a sparrow can still be hopping about after dark.
- The rowing boat is scenery: you fall through it into the sea.
- Touch and gamepad paths are written but untested.
- The lawn is sparse close to the camera, and the dock lamp post can block the view when you aim at a fish from directly behind it.
- Legs are IK'd to the terrain but the body does not lean on steep slopes, and the gallop was tuned from still frames, not from watching it move.

Decisions the brief left to me: Space is both hop and pounce (tap or hold) rather than two keys. Sneak is a toggle on C, because Ctrl+W closes the tab. A game is paused while any panel is open. The ending does not end the game.

## How I verified it

All in headless Chromium through the repository's Playwright recipe (`docs/TESTING.md`).

- `npm run check -- claude-fable-5-2` passes: loads, WebGL2 canvas, renders, animates, no external requests, no failed requests, no console errors.
- The game has a debug hook for deterministic playtests. With `?debug&manual` the frame loop stops and `window.__game.step(seconds)` advances the simulation at a fixed 60 Hz and draws once; `sim(seconds)` does the same without drawing. That removed wall-clock time from every test, which matters at 5 fps. Scripts driven this way:
  - title, start, walk, charge, release, land, with a screenshot at each stage;
  - a pounce at each of the ten animals and four objects from a sensible distance, confirming each one can be targeted and ends up in the bag (this found that prey was hearing the cat in mid-air and escaping every time, which is now fixed, and led to rebalancing pounce range against hearing ranges);
  - the mackerel caught from the dock;
  - carry, deliver, payout, the Tall One's note, shop purchases, wearing a coat and hat, journal, pause, buying the tin, the ending card, and returning to play (this found that E closed the shop and reopened it in the same frame);
  - all thirty sound calls invoked once to catch exceptions (this found a zero-volume ramp that Web Audio rejects);
  - the red dot caught three times at night;
  - a ten-minute simulated soak, running in circles through a full day and night, ending with sane positions and stable animal counts.
- Screenshots were looked at throughout: pose sheets of the cat alone (stand, sit, stalk, gallop phases, leap), then the island by day, dusk and night, and every screen of the UI. Several things were fixed from looking: the cat's head shape and proportions, washed-out grass, plants growing through the porch, a porch lamp that blew out the whole wall at night, HUD overlap.
- One environment trap worth knowing: headless Chromium grants pointer lock and then drops to under one frame per second. The repository's scripts now refuse pointer lock for every run.

To rebuild: there is no build step. `src/main.js` is the entry point and each file in `src/` starts with a comment saying what it is for.
